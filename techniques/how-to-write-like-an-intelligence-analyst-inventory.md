# How to Write Like an Intelligence Analyst — Page-by-Page Technique Inventory

## ANALYST PAGE 1
- **Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF)** — Get the critical information across to the 'consumer' (the specific reader for whom you're writing) as directly as possible: structure the product with the bottom line first, flipping the conventional organization of academic writing.
- **Know your audience and question** — The key to making the complex comprehensible is having in mind a specific audience and a very precise intelligence question for the analysis to tackle; always ask first: "Who is my audience and what specific question does it need answered?"
- **Avoid data dumps** — Data dumps and murky analysis almost always result from writing about a development without first fixing the audience and the precise question.
- **Balance evidence and judgement** — Writing like an intelligence analyst means balancing evidence and informed judgements.
- **Consider potential biases** — Factor potential biases into the analysis and the writing.
- **Concision and clarity** — Write for concision and clarity, the analyst's core writing habits.
- **Timeliness** — Timeliness is crucial: key consumers (the President, the National Security Council, senior military and executive branch leadership, some members of Congress; in commercial intelligence, corporate executives and staff charged with keeping employees and facilities safe) need the information fast, which is why the BLUF structure fits the audience.

## ANALYST PAGE 2
- **Impartiality Above All** — Intelligence analysis must be consistently impartial or risk losing the consumer's trust; stay cognizant of the potential for bias to influence analysis.
- **Distinguish Judgements from Evidence** — Clearly separate your judgements from the evidence presented: judgements are expressed in the BLUF and/or the impact statement that follows; evidence is then listed as succinctly as possible to directly support the analysis.
- **Judgement-Led Paragraphs** — Each supporting paragraph should lead with a judgement as the topic sentence, followed by the relevant evidence.
- **Identify and Acknowledge Evidentiary Bias** — Closely consider biases inherent in the ultimate source of the intelligence or that may have colored the information along the chain of acquisition — intentional (e.g., a politician's statement encompassing their party's perspective) or less obvious/diluted (e.g., an NGO report citing statistics from a third-party website); all quality analysis requires due diligence to identify and acknowledge potential bias.
- **Clear and Concise Style** — Write clearly and concisely: paragraphs focused around a single topic sentence, all serving as evidence to support the BLUF; word choice specific, simple, and straightforward.
- **KISS — Keep It Simple and Straightforward** — Use the KISS mnemonic to ensure efficiency and keep the audience from getting lost in complex sentences that could lead to confusion or misunderstanding.
- **Determine the BLUF from Expertise and Audience** — To determine the BLUF, the analyst must be an expert on the issues and understand his or her audience and what they need to know in order to make well-informed decisions.
- **Rewrite Dense Writing as an Intelligence Brief** — When stuck in a dense academic paper, rewrite it as an intelligence brief to define your judgements and your evidence and distill the author's or your points into their simplest, most logical order.
- **Inverted Structure** — Intelligence writing inverts the conventional structure of academic writing.
