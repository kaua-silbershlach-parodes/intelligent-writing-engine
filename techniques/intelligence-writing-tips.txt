This document outlines the six intelligence writing techniques. Each technique is stated first and explained in the lines that follow, in the order in which it is applied when writing.

The six techniques:

1. Clarity

Present findings clearly to avoid ambiguity or misinterpretation. Every word should be considered, questioned, and critiqued.

2. Conciseness

Eliminate unnecessary words and focus on the most essential information.

3. So What?

Answer why this is important, why it matters, and why it deserves the reader's time. If it doesn't answer the "so what?", the product is lacking.

4. Audience Analysis

Tailor the content to meet the intelligence requirements and expectations of the primary (and secondary) audience.

5. Avoid Observations

Observations aren't analytical. Determine whether your assessment is analytical and forward-leaning, and not just an observation.

6. Objectivity

Check your biases. Check them again. Now have a peer or colleague review your work. Avoid any biases that could skew your analysis or impact your or your organization's reputation.
